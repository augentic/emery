<h6 class="slide-eyebrow">PROPELLERHEAD · THE EXPOSURE <span class="eyebrow-num">03 / 07</span></h6>

# Where ungoverned AI projects come unstuck

<p class="slide-lead">A prompt can produce a convincing demo in an afternoon. The hidden cost arrives six months later when the business relies on it.</p>

<div class="exposure-grid">
<div class="cc-card">
<div class="card-eyebrow"><span class="card-num">01 · CONTINUITY</span><span>ORPHANED SYSTEMS</span></div>
<h3>Orphaned when the builder moves on</h3>
<p>Built by one person in a chat window with no shared architecture. When it breaks in production, nobody else knows how it works — or how to fix it safely.</p>
</div>
<div class="cc-card">
<div class="card-eyebrow"><span class="card-num">02 · INTENT</span><span>TRIBAL KNOWLEDGE</span></div>
<h3>The "why" disappears with the prompt</h3>
<p>Chat transcripts aren't specifications. Edge cases, compliance rules, and business decisions evaporate the moment the session closes.</p>
</div>
<div class="cc-card">
<div class="card-eyebrow"><span class="card-num">03 · SECURITY</span><span>UNBOUNDED ACCESS</span></div>
<h3>Quiet security and data exposure</h3>
<p>Hardcoded API keys, unreviewed dependencies, and live customer data sent to external models create risk that traditional reviews never see.</p>
</div>
<div class="cc-card">
<div class="card-eyebrow"><span class="card-num">04 · ECONOMICS</span><span>REWRITE TAX</span></div>
<h3>Cheap to start, expensive to rescue</h3>
<p>Teams end up paying twice: once for the prototype that business operations came to depend on, and again for the emergency engineering rewrite.</p>
</div>
</div>

<!--
Key message:
- Why does "cheap code" become so expensive? Because a demo that works on Friday afternoon looks identical on the surface to production software — until the business starts depending on it.
- Walk through the four failure modes:
  1. Continuity: one builder, one chat window, zero shared ownership.
  2. Intent: prompts disappear; nobody knows which rules in the generated code were deliberate business logic vs model hallucination.
  3. Security: secrets in repos, unvetted packages, and live data leaking into unapproved models.
  4. Economics: the "rewrite tax" when engineering has to drop everything to rescue an operational tool that silently became business-critical.
-->

<h6 class="slide-eyebrow">PROPELLERHEAD · LEGACY MODERNISATION <span class="eyebrow-num">06 / 07</span></h6>

# AI makes rewriting cheap enough<br>to be dangerous

<div class="modernisation-grid">
<div>
<div class="phl-tagline-box" style="grid-template-columns: 1fr; gap: 12px; margin-top: 4px; padding-left: 20px;">
<p class="lead-quote">When teams point an LLM directly at a legacy codebase and say <em>"translate this"</em>, they copy twenty years of accidental complexity into a brand-new language — and silently drop the edge cases that actually matter.</p>
<p style="font-size: 15.5px; line-height: 1.45;">Modernisation works when you separate <strong>what the system must do</strong> from <strong>how the old platform happened to do it.</strong></p>
</div>

<div class="proof-pair">
<div class="proof-mini-card">
<div class="proof-mini-head">
<span>SPEC-DRIVEN DELIVERY</span>
<img src="/images/emery-icon.svg" alt="" />
</div>
<h4>Emery</h4>
<p>Recovers platform-neutral specifications and scenarios from legacy code and docs.</p>
</div>
<div class="proof-mini-card">
<div class="proof-mini-head">
<span>WEBASSEMBLY RUNTIME</span>
<img src="/images/omnia-icon.svg" alt="" />
</div>
<h4>Omnia</h4>
<p>Runs rebuilt components inside explicit capability boundaries.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="modernisation-steps">
<div class="modernisation-step">
<div class="card-eyebrow" style="margin-bottom: 2px;"><span class="card-num">01 · RECOVER INTENT</span></div>
<h4>Mine the specification from existing code and docs</h4>
<p>Extract business behaviours, contracts, and test scenarios before writing a single line of replacement code.</p>
</div>
<div class="modernisation-step">
<div class="card-eyebrow" style="margin-bottom: 2px;"><span class="card-num">02 · RE-EXPRESS</span></div>
<h4>Leave legacy plumbing behind</h4>
<p>Keep the domain rules your business depends on; shed the obsolete frameworks, workarounds, and platform lock-in.</p>
</div>
<div class="modernisation-step">
<div class="card-eyebrow" style="margin-bottom: 2px;"><span class="card-num">03 · REPLACE SAFELY</span></div>
<h4>Verify behaviour slice by slice</h4>
<p>Prove the new component matches real recorded traffic and scenarios before retiring the legacy system.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<!--
Key message:
- The exact same discipline that makes citizen coding safe also solves one of the biggest traps in enterprise AI right now: naive legacy translation.
- Pointing an LLM at an old codebase and asking for a 1:1 translation just ports technical debt into a new syntax while losing subtle business rules.
- With Emery and Omnia, we do it in three deliberate steps:
  1. Recover intent: extract a platform-neutral specification of what the system actually does from the legacy code, tests, and documentation.
  2. Re-express: separate the business rules from the old framework plumbing.
  3. Replace safely: rebuild slice by slice on Omnia and verify against recorded production fixtures before cutting over.
-->
