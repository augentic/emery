<h6 class="slide-eyebrow">INTRODUCTION · WHY A MATURITY MODEL? <span class="eyebrow-num">02 / 18</span></h6>

# The riskiest moment is when<br>a prototype succeeds

<div class="opportunity-grid" style="margin-top: 10px;">
<div class="opportunity-left">
<div class="phl-tagline-box" style="grid-template-columns: 1fr; gap: 14px; margin-bottom: 18px;">
<p class="lead-quote">What starts as a personal script quietly becomes a team dependency — with no owner, no data boundary, and no support model.</p>
</div>
<div class="opportunity-rows">
<div class="opportunity-row">
<span class="opportunity-row-num">01</span>
<div>
<h4>Risk lives in the use, not the builder</h4>
<p>A personal prototype needs zero friction; an app running a department needs production assurance.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="opportunity-row">
<span class="opportunity-row-num">02</span>
<div>
<h4>Choose the zone that fits</h4>
<p>Inspired by the Agile Fluency® and Capability Maturity models, each level pairs a clear benefit with the investment required to sustain it.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="opportunity-row">
<span class="opportunity-row-num">03</span>
<div>
<h4>Clear triggers replace debates</h4>
<p>Observable thresholds for reach, data, and criticality tell you when an app must step up.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="opportunity-contrast">
<div class="contrast-box">
<span class="contrast-tag">Without a graduation path</span>
<h3>Success turns prototypes into shadow IT.</h3>
<p>Either every experiment is slowed down by enterprise gates on day one, or successful prototypes quietly run critical operations with zero support.</p>
</div>
<div class="contrast-box governed">
<span class="contrast-tag">The Maturity Model</span>
<h3>Move fast early. Harden as value is proven.</h3>
<p>Keep ~75% of experiments lightweight in a safe sandbox, and invest data-plane and engineering rigour only as apps earn adoption.</p>
<div class="contrast-pills">
<span class="contrast-pill">Proportionate controls</span>
<span class="contrast-pill">Observable gates</span>
<span class="contrast-pill">Clean handover</span>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<!--
Section 0 — Introduction (Why a Maturity Model?):
- In our Citizen Coding framework, we show why domain experts need a safe lane to build with AI.
- This model answers the operational question: how do apps progress from a raw idea on someone's laptop to a supported enterprise asset — and how do we know when an app needs to move up?
- Like Diana Larsen and James Shore's Agile Fluency Model, higher isn't automatically better for every app. You buy the ticket for the zone the business actually needs.
-->

Overview of the model:
- Notice the visual staircase: each level stacks a new capability layer underneath the citizen builder.
- Also notice how transitions work (bottom bar):
  - An EXIT TRIGGER is demand-driven: users, data, or criticality outgrow the safety boundary of the current level.
  - An ENTRY GATE is supply-driven: the enabling investment (Codex, Data Plane, or Spec-led Engineering Handover) required to enter the next level safely.
- In a healthy portfolio, ~75% of apps live happily at Level 1, ~20% at Level 2, and ~5% graduate to Level 3.

<h6 class="slide-eyebrow">MATURITY LEVEL 0 · OUTLINE <span class="eyebrow-num">05 / 18</span></h6>

# Level 0: Raw &amp; Unmanaged

<p class="slide-lead">Ad-hoc prototypes built in personal tools or unguided chat — valuable as a discovery signal, unsafe to share.</p>

<div class="level-outline-grid">
<div class="level-profile-card is-l0">
<div>
<div class="level-big-tag"><span>MATURITY LEVEL 0</span><span>·</span><span>INITIAL</span></div>
<h2>Unguided Exploration</h2>
<p>An individual solving an immediate friction point on their own machine.</p>
</div>
<div class="level-meta-list">
<div class="level-meta-row">
<span class="level-meta-label">BENEFIT</span>
<span class="level-meta-val">Zero-cost problem discovery</span>
</div>
<div class="level-meta-row">
<span class="level-meta-label">INVESTMENT</span>
<span class="level-meta-val">None (ad-hoc individual effort)</span>
</div>
<div class="level-meta-row">
<span class="level-meta-label">GOVERNANCE STANCE</span>
<span class="level-meta-val">Treat as a signal — onboard to Level 1</span>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="level-features-2x2">
<div class="level-feature-box is-dashed">
<div class="level-feature-head"><span>01 · WORKSPACE</span><span>ISOLATED</span></div>
<h3>Personal Tools &amp; Devices</h3>
<p>Built in consumer AI chat, local scripts, or unmanaged spreadsheets with no version control.</p>
</div>

<div class="level-feature-box is-dashed">
<div class="level-feature-head"><span>02 · ARCHITECTURE</span><span>HIGH VARIANCE</span></div>
<h3>Unconstrained Stack</h3>
<p>Every prompt invents its own framework, packages, and authentication shortcuts.</p>
</div>

<div class="level-feature-box is-dashed">
<div class="level-feature-head"><span>03 · DATA ACCESS</span><span>UNBOUNDED</span></div>
<h3>Manual Extracts &amp; Keys</h3>
<p>Relies on pasted text, CSV exports, or personal API keys embedded in code.</p>
</div>

<div class="level-feature-box is-dashed">
<div class="level-feature-head"><span>04 · CONTINUITY</span><span>SINGLE POINT</span></div>
<h3>100% Maker-Dependent</h3>
<p>Intent lives only in a chat window. If the builder moves on, the tool breaks.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
